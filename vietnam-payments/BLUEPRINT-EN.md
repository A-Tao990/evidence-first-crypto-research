# Bringing Crypto into Vietnam's Everyday Payments: A One-Page Blueprint

*v0.2 (Nov 2026). Written by an independent observer based in Vietnam. Drafted with AI assistance (Claude); the core judgments come from the author's own market observation. This is not legal advice.*

**How to read the labels:** **[Observed]** = the author's first-hand observation or earlier market research. **[Reported]** = public sources, listed at the end; quality varies. **[Hypothesis]** = reasoning not yet verified.

---

## 1. The gap

- Merchants where the author lives and shops accept **bank QR, MoMo and ZaloPay**. The author has not seen a single merchant offering an exchange-based payment option. **[Observed]**
- Most exchange-issued virtual cards do not work in Vietnam. Where they can be added to PayPal or Google Pay, they still do not fit local habits: users would have to install new apps, apply for cards and submit documents. **[Observed]**
- VietQR processes roughly 15 million transfers a day and is accepted by about 85% of merchants. **[Reported: Banxa, July 2026]**
- Digital assets are recognised as property but are **not legal tender and may not be used as a lawful means of payment** for goods and services. **[Reported: Law on Digital Technology Industry, via Circle, Oct 2026]**

Crypto users are a small minority in Vietnam. Receiving Vietnamese dong, however, works for every merchant and every customer.

## 2. The thesis

> **Do not ask Vietnamese users to adopt a new payment method. Put crypto behind the rails they already use.**

Merchants keep receiving dong. Users keep their apps. Platforms move into the regulated perimeter.

## 3. The target flow

```
Crypto holder
   │  sells on a LICENSED Vietnamese exchange (dong settlement)
   ▼
Bank-issued VND virtual card / account  (licensed Vietnamese bank)
   │  linked through the wallet's standard bank-linking flow
   ▼
MoMo / ZaloPay / VietQR  →  Merchant receives VND
```

- Crypto appears only at the **top-up** step, which matches the pilot's requirement that licensed exchanges settle in dong. **[Reported: Resolution 05/2025/NQ-CP]**
- A card issued by a Vietnamese bank should link to local wallets through their existing bank-linking process, without a separate compliance negotiation. **[Hypothesis — each wallet's supported-bank list and any extra approvals must be confirmed]**
- Foreign-BIN exchange cards are a poor fit: one (older) guide says ZaloPay supports only domestically issued cards. **[Reported, needs verification]**

## 4. Who can build it

- Only Vietnamese enterprises may apply for an exchange licence; foreign ownership is capped at **49%**. The framework clearly favours local companies. **[Reported]**
- Large foreign exchanges therefore have a realistic role as **minority shareholders, technology providers or liquidity partners**, not licence holders. **[Hypothesis]**
- As of the latest reports (Aug–Oct 2026), **no exchange holds a final licence.** Five applicants passed preliminary review: VIX, Techcom (TCEX), CAEX, SCEX and Vietnam Digital Assets. Source lists differ slightly. **[Reported]**
- Reported ties: CAEX belongs to the VPBank ecosystem, with HashKey as exclusive core technology partner and an investment from OKX Ventures and HashKey Capital (10 April 2026); SCEX (formerly LPEX) reportedly counts LPBank and Sacombank among its shareholders; TCEX sits in the Techcom ecosystem. **[Reported]**
- CAEX, SCEX and TCEX announced they passed Level-4 information-security assessments on 22–23 October 2026; licences are still pending. **[Reported]**

## 5. What each party gains

| Party | Gain |
|---|---|
| Merchants | No change; they keep receiving VND |
| Users | Existing apps, no new card applications |
| Exchanges & banks | A ready-made local user base and real spending use cases |
| Regulators | Trading on licensed platforms, VND settlement, traceable flows |

## 6. Open questions

1. Will banks issue such virtual cards or accounts for licensed exchanges? *Unknown.*
2. Do MoMo and ZaloPay currently allow this kind of linking, and with what approvals? *Unknown.*
3. When will the first final licences be granted, and who launches first? *Unknown.*
4. How will anti-money-laundering rules, limits and KYC shape the user experience? *Unknown.*
